Local reality

Three things decide whether a local site earns its keep. None of them is decoration.

What is actually different about a Delhi website

  1. 01

    Most of it is read on a phone, on mobile data

    Someone standing outside a shop or on a bus is not waiting for a hero animation. A page that loads in about a second, says what you do, and lets a thumb tap a phone number does more for a Delhi business than a large banner photograph.

  2. 02

    The map result sits above the website

    For "dentist near me" or "interior painter Dwarka", the map pack appears first. A verified Google Business Profile with real reviews and matching address, phone and hours does part of the job the website cannot, so the two have to agree with each other word for word.

  3. 03

    Search is in English, the conversation may not be

    People usually type their search in English and then speak Hindi or Hinglish once they call. A site that states the trade plainly in English, with opening hours and an address a customer can repeat to a driver, serves both halves of that.

Five things every site leaves with

  1. 01

    One page, one action

    Each page asks for a single thing: call, message, book or visit. Two competing buttons halve both.

  2. 02

    Contact details where a thumb can reach them

    The phone number is a tap-to-call link, the address opens in Maps, and the enquiry form works without JavaScript tricks that break on a cheap handset.

  3. 03

    Tested at 360px, not just resized

    Layouts are checked at the width of a small Android phone. No sideways scrolling, no tap target under 44px, no text that turns into a grey blur at 3G speeds.

  4. 04

    Domain, HTTPS and a working form

    Your domain points at the site, http and www redirect to one address, the padlock works, and the form has been tested by sending a real message from an outside email.

  5. 05

    A handover you can act on

    Where the files live, which accounts exist, what each one costs a year, and what a change costs before you ask for one.

Being found

A website is half of it. These are the other four things that move a Delhi business up the local results.

What to do after launch, in order

  1. 01

    Claim and verify the Google Business Profile

    Correct category, real service area, real hours, ten photos. This is what appears in the map pack, and no amount of on-page work replaces it.

  2. 02

    Make the name, address and phone identical everywhere

    Website, profile, invoices, directories, the signboard. Inconsistent details are read as different businesses, and the weaker one is dropped.

  3. 03

    Ask three customers for a review in the first month

    Ask when the work is delivered and they are pleased, send the direct review link, and reply to each one. Reviews are the only ranking input you can still influence after launch.

  4. 04

    Then add pages that answer real questions

    "How much does a website cost in Delhi", "who hosts it", "what happens after launch" are all queries with buyers behind them. Each honest answer is a page that can rank and be quoted by an AI assistant.

What none of this does is work overnight. A new domain takes weeks to be indexed and months to rank for a competitive phrase, and anyone promising a first-page position in a week is selling advertising rather than search.

How much does a website cost in Delhi?

A one-page site is $249 / ₹23,400, five pages is $549 / ₹51,600, and five pages with a blog is $899 / ₹84,500. Those are one-off prices for the build, fixed in writing before work starts. How long does a website take to build?

One page takes 1 to 2 weeks from approved quote to launch. Five pages takes 3 to 4 weeks. The clock starts when the text and photos are ready, so late content moves the launch date by the same amount.

Do you only work with Delhi businesses?

No. The studio is in New Delhi and the work is done over email and video, so the process is the same for a business in Noida, Gurugram or another country. Delhi clients get no different process and no slower one.

Do I need a Hindi version of my website?

Only if your customers search in Hindi. Most Delhi searches for services happen in English, but a trade serving an area where customers prefer Hindi gains from a Hindi page. Each language is a separate page with its own URL and is priced as extra pages, not as a translation add-on.

Who owns the domain and the hosting?

You do, in your own accounts. Creating them before the build means nobody can hold the site hostage later, and the yearly fees stay yours to control.

Can you repair or rebuild an existing website?

Yes. Send the URL and what is wrong with it. The reply says whether a repair or a rebuild costs less, and either way the price is fixed before work begins.
