Hosting

Website hosting explained without the jargon

Three separate things get sold together and confused constantly. Here is what each one does, what it should cost you, and whose name should be on the account.

By Manoj Kumar Published 9 September 2026 4 min read

Short answer

A domain is your address, hosting is the space where your files live, and an SSL certificate is what makes the padlock and the https appear. You need all three. Register the domain and hosting in your own accounts, so a change of designer never becomes a change of website.

The three parts

What you are actually buying
PartWhat it doesBilledWhose account
DomainThe name people type, such as yourbusiness.comYearlyYours
HostingStores the files and serves them to visitorsMonthly or yearlyYours
SSL certificateEncrypts the connection and shows httpsUsually included freeYours

A domain and a host are not the same purchase even when one company sells both. Losing access to one is annoying; losing access to both is how businesses lose their site.

Why HTTPS is not optional

Browsers mark plain http pages as "not secure", which is a bad first impression on a page asking for a phone number. Google also documents HTTPS as a lightweight ranking signal and recommends it for every site. Certificates are free through services such as Let's Encrypt, and most modern hosts enable them by default. If a host charges extra for a certificate, that tells you something about the rest of the service.

Once HTTPS is on, make sure http and the www version both redirect to one address. Two versions of the same page splitting their signals is a self-inflicted wound.

Static hosting versus WordPress hosting

A static site is a folder of files. Any host that can serve files will do, including free tiers from Cloudflare Pages and GitHub Pages, and the site will survive traffic spikes because the files are cached around the world. There is no database, so there is nothing to patch.

WordPress needs PHP, a database and usually email. That means a more expensive host, and a host that takes backups seriously. Budget for the security and maintenance work as well as the hosting bill.

What to check before you sign up

  1. HTTPS included and renewing automatically.
  2. HTTP/2 or HTTP/3 support, which every mainstream host now offers.
  3. A CDN, or servers close to your customers.
  4. Backups you can restore yourself, not just "we take backups".
  5. Support that answers a technical question in under a day.
  6. Easy export: can you download the site files and move them?
  7. No surprise renewal price after the first year.

Who should own the accounts

You. Register the domain with your email, add your designer as a user if the host supports it, and keep the renewal card up to date. A designer who insists on owning the domain is creating a hold over you that nobody needs. On my projects the client creates the accounts and shares access, so the site is never held hostage by either side.

Keep a note of three things somewhere you will find them in a year: the registrar, the host, and the email address used for both. That note is worth more than the hosting receipt.

What hosting does not fix

A faster server will not rescue a page with a 6MB hero photo, and it will not make a site appear in search results. Hosting sets the floor for speed. The images, fonts and scripts set the ceiling, which is covered in how to make a business website load in under two seconds.
