How to build a business website, in the right order

Most websites go over budget for one reason: the design is drawn before anyone has decided what the site has to sell and written the words that do it.

By Manoj Kumar Published 19 May 2026 3 min read

Short answer

Work in this order: decide the one action a visitor must take, buy the domain in your own name, write the page list, write the content, then design and build, then host and launch. Anything built before the content exists has to be rebuilt once real sentences arrive.

1. Decide the one action

A website has one job: get the visitor to do the thing that makes money. Book an appointment, request a quote, call the number, reserve a table. Write that action on a piece of paper. Every page either moves a visitor towards it or is decoration.

Two competing actions halve both. If a roofer wants calls and quote form submissions, that is fine, because those are the same intent. A roofer who also wants people to read a blog, download an ebook and follow an Instagram account is describing four businesses.

2. Buy the domain in your own name

Register the domain in your own account, with your own email address and payment method. A designer or agency should be able to point the domain at a new host, but should not own it. Domain problems are almost always ownership problems, and they arrive at the worst possible moment.

Pick a short name that a customer can say on the phone and spell correctly after hearing it once. Your business name with your city is usually enough. .com is not mandatory for a local business if the country domain is the one customers recognise.

3. Write the page list

Five pages cover most local businesses: home, services, about, work or gallery, and contact. One service gets one page, because a page that covers roofing, gutters and solar panels is competing with itself for the same search terms.

The page list is the document that decides the price, so agree it in writing before design starts. My own packages are built on it: one page from $249 / ₹23,400 and five pages from $549 / ₹51,600.

4. Write the content before the design

This is the step most projects skip and the reason most projects run late. Write the heading, the opening answer and the main body of every page in plain text first. When the words exist, layout decisions stop being guesses: a page with four short services and a page with one long story need completely different treatment.

Two rules keep it quick. Answer the question in the first two sentences rather than warming up. Put the real number in the sentence: how many years, how many jobs, what it costs, how long it takes.

5. Design and build

Design against the content, at phone width first, and check three things before you approve anything: body text of at least 16px, text contrast of at least 4.5:1 against its background, and tap targets at least 44px high. These are accessibility basics and they also happen to be what makes a site usable with one thumb.

Keep the type to two families and the palette to four colours. Fewer choices read as deliberate; more choices read as an accident.

6. Host and launch

Connect the domain, confirm HTTPS works, and test the contact form by sending a real message from an email address outside your own network. Then submit the site to Google Search Console and Bing Webmaster Tools, and set up a Google Business Profile if you serve a place. Hosting basics are covered in website hosting explained without the jargon.

What it costs in time

The build itself is the fast part. The slow part is content, and it is the part only the owner can do. If photos and text arrive a week late, the launch is a week late, no matter how quick the developer is. Plan the content stage before you book the build stage.
