AI search

What AEO and GEO mean, and what they change for a business

Two acronyms, one practical question: when a customer asks an AI assistant for a recommendation, does your business get named? Here is how those answers are built, what genuinely affects them, and what to ignore when someone sells you a guarantee.

By Manoj Kumar Published 12 November 2026 Updated 18 November 2026 6 min read

Short answer

AEO (answer engine optimisation) is the work of making your pages easy for an AI tool to quote accurately. GEO (generative engine optimisation) is the wider job of making the business itself recognisable enough to be mentioned at all. Neither is a switch you turn on: both are mostly good SEO plus plain writing, consistent business details, and other websites that mention you.

Why this appeared at all

A search result gives a customer ten links and a decision to make. An AI assistant gives one paragraph and a few sources. For a local business that is a meaningful shift: being the fourth link is worth something, being absent from the paragraph is worth nothing.

The two acronyms describe the two ways you can end up in that paragraph. AEO is about whether the tool can read your page and state your prices, hours and service area correctly. GEO is about whether the tool knows you exist when it starts assembling the answer, which depends on mentions beyond your own site: directories, local press, associations, supplier pages, reviews.

How an AI answer is actually assembled

Most assistants that cite sources work in three steps, and each one is a place where a business can be lost.

Three steps, and what each one needs from you
StepWhat happensWhat it needs from you
1. RetrieveThe tool searches an index, much like a search engine does, for pages that might answer the question.A site that crawlers can reach: no accidental blocking, a sitemap, fast pages, text in HTML rather than only in images.
2. RankThe candidate pages are scored for how well they answer this specific question.A page whose heading matches the question, with the answer written in the first paragraph rather than after three of introduction.
3. ComposeThe model writes an answer and attaches sources, quoting what it can verify.Specific, checkable facts: prices, areas, hours, qualifications, dates. Vague claims cannot be quoted.

That order matters. If step one fails, nothing else helps. Most of the "AI visibility" work sold today is really step one and step three: getting crawled, and being quotable.

What genuinely moves the needle

These are the levers I have seen hold up, in rough order of effort against effect. The full set of services and prices is on the AEO and GEO page.

  1. Be a consistent entity. The same business name, address, phone number and description on your site, your Google Business Profile, LinkedIn, and the two or three directories that matter in your trade. Conflicting details give a model nothing solid to cite, and it will choose a competitor with cleaner facts.
  2. Answer the question on its own page. One page per service, headed with the customer's question, answered in the first two sentences. A generic "Services" page covering six trades answers none of them well. This is the same advice as the local SEO basics, applied to questions rather than keywords.
  3. Publish numbers. "Affordable" is not quotable. "A one-page site costs from $249 / ₹23,400 and takes one to two weeks" is. Price ranges, turnaround times, areas served, warranty length, years in business: each specific fact is a sentence an assistant can use and attribute.
  4. Make the page machine-readable. Clean headings, real tables instead of screenshots of tables, Organization and LocalBusiness structured data, a visible author and a date. There is no special AI markup to add; Google documents no such requirement, and the schema that helps is the ordinary kind.
  5. Get mentioned elsewhere. A citation on a local news site, a trade association listing or a supplier's stockist page does more for GEO than another paragraph on your homepage, because it is evidence from outside your own mouth.

What does not work, however it is packaged

Keyword stuffing for AI is still keyword stuffing. A file named llms.txt is a proposal, not a standard any major assistant relies on, so treat it as harmless rather than essential. Mass-produced FAQ sections that repeat the same question in eight wordings make a page worse, not more quotable. And a monthly report showing "AI mentions" with no fixed question set behind it cannot be checked by anyone, including the person selling it.

The one promise to refuse outright is a guaranteed position in ChatGPT or Perplexity answers. Answers vary between runs, even on the same day, because the retrieval step and the model both change. Honest tracking means a fixed set of questions, run repeatedly, compared against the business's own earlier numbers.

A thirty-minute check you can do this week

  1. Write ten questions a real customer would type, each with your town or area in it.
  2. Ask all ten in ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini and Copilot. Note for each: named, cited with a link, or absent.
  3. Ask the same ten again two days later. The difference is the noise floor. Any change smaller than that is not a result.
  4. Open the pages that were cited. Compare their headings, price detail and dates with yours. That is your to-do list, and it is usually shorter than expected.
  5. Fix the facts first: name, address, phone, prices, hours, on every profile you control.

If the site cannot be crawled or loads in five seconds, stop there and fix that before spending anything on AEO. It is the cheapest step and it gates all the others.

Three questions I get asked

Is AEO different from SEO?

They overlap heavily. Both need a crawlable site, pages that answer a real question and a business that other sites mention. AEO adds work on the shape of the page: a direct answer near the top, specific numbers, a dated author and structured data an AI tool can read without guessing.

Can anyone guarantee a business gets cited by ChatGPT?

No. The models change, the retrieval index changes and the answers vary between runs, so nobody can promise a position the way a search rank can be tracked. What can be measured is how often a business is mentioned or cited across a fixed set of questions, compared with its own baseline.

How do I check whether AI tools mention my business today?

Write ten questions a customer would actually type, including the town or area you serve. Ask each one in ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini and Copilot, and record whether you are named, cited with a link, or missing. That record is the baseline, and repeating it on another day shows how much the answer moves on its own.
