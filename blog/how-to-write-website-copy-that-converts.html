Copy

How to write website copy that gets a call

Visitors do not read a page, they scan it for the answer to one question: can this business do what I need, near me, at a price I accept?

By Manoj Kumar Published 1 August 2026 4 min read

Short answer

Answer the customer's question in the first two sentences under the heading, then give the specifics: the service, the area, the price or range, the next step. Cut adjectives that cannot be checked, keep paragraphs to two or three lines, and repeat one clear action on every screen. A page that reads like a price list with reasons beats a page that reads like a brochure.

The first fifty words do the work

Someone searching "emergency plumber New Delhi" wants to know three things before they scroll: do you cover my area, can you come today, and what will it cost. Put those in the opening paragraph. The story about the family business can come after the phone number.

This is also the paragraph an AI answer engine is most likely to quote, because it is self-contained. Write it as if it will be read alone, without the rest of the page.

Replace every vague claim with a fact

Illustrative rewrites, not client copy
VagueSpecific
High quality workmanshipEvery roof is inspected after 12 months, free
Fast responseCalls answered 8am to 6pm, Monday to Saturday
Affordable pricesRoof repairs from $180, quoted before work starts
Experienced teamEstablished May 2026, three client sites live
Trusted local businessServing New Delhi and 20km around it since [year]

If you cannot fill the right-hand column, that is not a writing problem. It is a missing fact, and inventing one is how sites end up with reviews nobody wrote and numbers nobody can check.

One page, one action

The page should ask for one thing: call, book, request a quote. A page with a phone number, a newsletter box, four social icons and a chat bubble splits attention four ways. Keep the secondary options in the footer.

Put the action where a thumb rests. On a phone that is the lower half of the screen, so a persistent call bar or a button repeated after the main sections works better than a single link at the top.

Format for scanning

  • One idea per paragraph, two or three lines long.
  • Headings as the questions people actually ask, not as abstract labels.
  • Lists for anything with three or more items, especially prices and inclusions.
  • Line length around 60 to 75 characters. Wider is tiring; narrower is choppy.
  • Bold for the one number or phrase a skimmer needs, not for whole sentences.

Words to delete

"Passionate", "bespoke", "seamless", "cutting-edge", "unlock", "elevate", "one-stop shop", "trusted partner", "in today's digital world". None of these mean anything to a customer with a leaking roof. If a sentence could appear unchanged on a competitor's site, it is not about your business and it can go.

Write the page in this order

  1. The heading: what this page is about, in the customer's words.
  2. The answer: two sentences, covering the service, area and price.
  3. Proof: photos of real work, numbers you can defend, a named person.
  4. Detail: process, inclusions, exclusions, questions.
  5. The action, repeated at the bottom.
